# Stoicism 101 — landing

A dark editorial landing page for a philosophy course, built on high-contrast serif display
type, sculptural imagery, and a great deal of empty space.

## What makes it work

Type sits **behind** the object. The display word is partially occluded by the bust in front of
it, which forces the eye to reconstruct the word and instantly reads as art direction rather than
as layout. It is one z-index decision and it does more than any effect could.

Second: gold appears only at ornament scale — a logo, a glow, a gilded surface, a dot indicator.
It never sets type and never fills a button. A single restrained metallic against deep navy is
the entire luxury signal.

## Layout

A vertical rail down the left edge carries rotated section labels — small, muted, uppercase,
one per section. It runs the full page and gives structure without any visible grid.

The hero is a centred pedestal composition: object on a white disc, symmetrical, with supporting
content pushed to the outer thirds — a text block left, a lesson selector right. The display word
spans nearly the full width behind it.

A philosopher carousel follows: three busts on a shallow connecting arc, with circular arrow
controls at either end. The arc is the only curved element on the page and it earns its place by
implying rotation.

The practices section is centred serif heading, then a horizontal tab row with an underlined
active item, then a two-column split — theory left, numbered lesson right, separated by a hairline
with the lesson number sitting on it.

The closing section breaks symmetry: a full-height sculpture bleeding off the left edge, text
right, heading set flush against the figure.

## Typography

Display and all headings are serif. High-contrast didone for the hero word, letterspaced wide and
set light — at that size, a light weight reads as confident rather than thin.

Body is a small geometric sans at ~11px with very generous leading (~1.8) and muted colour. It is
deliberately quiet; nothing in the sans ever competes with a serif.

The rule worth stealing: **sans never sets a heading here.** Voice belongs entirely to the serif,
and that single constraint is what keeps a very dark page from feeling like a dashboard.

## Colour

Deep navy, never pure black — the blue cast is what stops it reading as a terminal. Surfaces
step up by a few points of lightness only.

Gold at roughly 2% coverage: mark, glow, gilding, active dot. Body copy is a muted slate-blue.
Near-white is cool-tinted to match the base rather than fighting it.

## Shape & depth

Pill-shaped outlined buttons — thin border, no fill, small caps. Otherwise almost no shape
language: no cards, no panels, no shadows. Depth comes from the photography and from the white
pedestal disc, not from elevation.

## Motion

Implied: carousel arrows, tab underline, dot indicators. Nothing suggests animation beyond state.

## Anti-patterns it avoids

- No cards. A content-heavy page with zero card wrappers.
- No sans-serif headings
- No gold text, no gold buttons — ornament scale only
- No pure black, no pure white
- No gradient anywhere; depth is photographic

## Reproducing this without the imagery

Harder than the travel entry, and worth being honest about. The sculptures are the subject, not
decoration — the pedestal composition is empty without them, and stock photography will not
supply gold-lit classical busts on transparent backgrounds.

What survives placeholders: the left rail of rotated labels, the serif-only heading rule, the
type-behind-object layering, the gold restraint, the airy vertical rhythm, the pill buttons.

What needs real assets: three to four cut-out sculptures with a consistent light direction and
transparent backgrounds. This is a generation problem, not a stock problem.
